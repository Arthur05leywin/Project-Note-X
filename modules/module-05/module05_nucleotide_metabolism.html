Nucleotide Structure

A Nucleotide = Nitrogenous base + Pentose sugar + Phosphate group. Remove phosphate → Nucleoside. Remove sugar → just the base.
Purines
Bicyclic: pyrimidine fused to imidazole ring
  • Adenine (A) — amino group at C6
  • Guanine (G) — keto group at C6, amino at C2
  • Found in both DNA and RNA
  • Larger molecules (2 rings)
Pyrimidines
Monocyclic: single 6-membered ring
  • Cytosine (C) — both DNA & RNA
  • Thymine (T) — DNA only (methyl group at C5)
  • Uracil (U) — RNA only (no methyl group)
  • Smaller molecules (1 ring)
Sugars
Pentose in N-glycosidic bond (β-configuration)
  • Ribose — in RNA nucleotides (2'-OH present)
  • Deoxyribose — in DNA (2'-H, no OH)
  • Base attached to C1' (N-glycosidic bond)
  • Phosphate on 5'-OH
Key Bonds
Connecting components
  • N-glycosidic bond: base → sugar (C1')
  • Phosphoester bond: sugar C5' → phosphate
  • 3'–5' phosphodiester: links nucleotides in chain
  • DNA more stable: 2'-H (not OH) → alkali resistant
Component Purine Pyrimidine
Ring structure Bicyclic (double ring) Monocyclic (single ring)
Members Adenine, Guanine Cytosine, Thymine (DNA), Uracil (RNA)
Pairing (Watson-Crick) A pairs with T (2 H-bonds); G pairs with C (3 H-bonds) Paired with purines
De novo synthesis On pre-formed ribose-5-P scaffold (IMP → AMP/GMP) Ring built first, then attached to ribose
End product of catabolism Uric acid (humans) CO₂ + NH₃ + β-alanine/β-aminoisobutyrate
Salvage pathway enzyme HGPRT (HG), APRT (A) Not prominent (minor UPRTase)
Mnemonic — Which Bases in DNA vs RNA
DNA: "A TiGht Cell" = A, T, G, C (Thymine only in DNA)
RNA: "A UGly Cell" = A, U, G, C (Uracil replaces Thymine)

Key EQ: "DNA is alkali resistant, RNA is alkali labile" — because RNA has 2'-OH which forms cyclic phosphodiester intermediate under alkaline hydrolysis; DNA lacks 2'-OH so cannot.

Regulation of Purine De Novo Synthesis
  • PRPP synthetase: inhibited by AMP, GMP, IMP (end-product inhibition)
  • Glutamine:PRPP amidotransferase (RLS): inhibited by AMP + GMP together (synergistic feedback)
  • Cross-regulation: AMP synthesis needs GTP; GMP synthesis needs ATP → reciprocal balance
  • In Gout: hyperactive PRPP synthetase or mutant HGPRT → excess purine → excess uric acid
Site of de novo synthesis: Liver primarily (can export nucleotides to other tissues). Brain and muscle have limited capacity → depend on salvage.

Clinical Link — Orotic Aciduria

Type I (classic): Deficiency of UMP synthase (OPRT + ODCase). Orotate accumulates → excreted in urine. Megaloblastic anaemia (pyrimidines needed for DNA). Treatment: Uridine supplementation (bypasses block, also provides feedback inhibition of CPS II).

Type II: Deficiency of ODCase only (less severe).

Distinguish from: Orotic aciduria in urea cycle disorders (OTC deficiency) — excess carbamoyl phosphate overflows into pyrimidine pathway → orotate ↑. But NO megaloblastic anaemia (UMP synthase intact, can process the orotate).

Salvage Pathways

Salvage = Recycle pre-formed bases or nucleosides back to nucleotides. Energetically cheaper than de novo. Crucial in tissues with limited de novo capacity (brain, RBCs).
Purine Salvage — HGPRT & APRT ⭐ PYQ 2018, 2025
Purine Salvage Reactions
Free bases (Hypoxanthine, Guanine, Adenine) from turnover or diet
↓
Enzyme: HGPRT Hypoxanthine-Guanine Phosphoribosyl Transferase
Hypoxanthine + PRPP → IMP
Guanine + PRPP → GMP
Enzyme: APRT Adenine Phosphoribosyl Transferase
Adenine + PRPP → AMP

Why Salvage Matters:

  • Brain relies almost exclusively on salvage (low de novo)
  • Less ATP consumed than de novo synthesis
  • Also recovers bases from nucleic acid degradation
  • Maintains nucleotide pool balance

When Salvage Fails:

  • HGPRT deficiency → Lesch-Nyhan Syndrome
  • Hypoxanthine/Guanine cannot be recycled → converted to uric acid instead → hyperuricaemia
  • Also: PRPP accumulates → drives de novo synthesis faster → more purines → more uric acid
  • Brain cannot compensate → severe neurological features
Adenosine Deaminase (ADA) — Critical Enzyme

↓ ADA (Adenosine Deaminase)

↓ Purine Nucleoside Phosphorylase

ADA Deficiency → SCID (Severe Combined Immunodeficiency)

ADA absent → dATP accumulates (deoxyadenosine → dAMP → dADP → dATP).

dATP: inhibits ribonucleotide reductase → blocks ALL dNTP synthesis → DNA synthesis halts in rapidly dividing lymphocytes (T & B cells) → lymphocyte death → profound immunodeficiency.

Treatment: Enzyme replacement (PEG-ADA), gene therapy (first successful gene therapy target).

Why Uric Acid (Not Allantoin) in Humans?

Most mammals have uricase → converts uric acid → allantoin (more soluble). Humans and great apes lost uricase during evolution (gene silenced). Result: uric acid is the final product and can crystallise in tissues.

06

Gout & Hyperuricaemia

Gout = Deposition of monosodium urate (MSU) crystals in joints (especially 1st metatarsophalangeal joint) + soft tissues (tophi) due to sustained hyperuricaemia.
Causes of Hyperuricaemia ⭐ Core

↑ Uric Acid Production:

  • HGPRT deficiency (Lesch-Nyhan)
  • PRPP synthetase overactivity
  • Increased cell turnover (leukaemia, lymphoma, psoriasis)
  • Von Gierke's disease (G6P → PRPP overproduction)
  • High purine diet (red meat, organ meats, shellfish, beer)
  • Alcohol (lactate competes with urate for renal excretion + increased purine catabolism)
  • Tumour lysis syndrome (chemo)

↓ Uric Acid Excretion:

  • Renal insufficiency
  • Low-dose aspirin (competes with urate at proximal tubule)
  • Thiazide diuretics
  • Lactic acidosis (lactate competes at renal tubule)
  • Cyclosporine
Alcohol & Gout

Alcohol → lactate (NADH ↑ in liver → pyruvate→lactate) → lactate competes with urate for renal tubular secretion → ↓ urate excretion → ↑ serum urate → precipitates gout attack.

Also: Beer has guanosine (purine precursor).

Von Gierke's disease is associated with hyperuricaemia.PYQ 2012, 2017
Standard Exam Answer Draft:
  1. Primary Defect: Von Gierke's disease (Type Ia Glycogen Storage Disease) is caused by a deficiency of Glucose-6-phosphatase, blocking the final step of both glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis.
  2. G6P Accumulation: Glucose-6-phosphate (G6P) accumulates and is shunted into the Pentose Phosphate Pathway, generating excess Ribose-5-phosphate → elevated PRPP (phosphoribosyl pyrophosphate).
  3. De Novo Purine Surge: PRPP is the committed substrate for de novo purine synthesis. Its excess drives accelerated purine production, greatly increasing flux through the catabolic pathway.
  4. Uric Acid Overproduction: The purine catabolism pathway terminates in uric acid (via xanthine oxidase), causing hyperuricaemia.
  5. Renal Competition: Lactic acidosis (from blocked gluconeogenesis) causes lactate to compete with urate for renal tubular secretion, further reducing uric acid excretion and worsening hyperuricaemia.
  6. Clinical Result: Gout and/or uric acid nephrolithiasis develop alongside the typical features of hypoglycaemia and hepatomegaly.

Lesch-Nyhan & ADA Deficiency

Lesch-Nyhan Syndrome ⭐ Advance Topic
  • Deficiency: HGPRT (complete absence)
  • Inheritance: X-linked recessive (affects males)
  • Gene: HPRT1 gene on X chromosome

Biochemical Consequences:

  • Hypoxanthine & Guanine cannot be salvaged
  • ↑↑ PRPP (no consumption by HGPRT) → drives de novo synthesis
  • ↑↑ Purine catabolism → uric acid

Clinical Features (the triad):

  • Severe gout + hyperuricaemia (urate nephropathy, tophi)
  • Neurological: intellectual disability, choreoathetosis, spastic cerebral palsy
  • Compulsive self-mutilation (lip/finger biting — pathognomonic!)
Why Neurological Features?

Brain depends almost entirely on purine salvage (negligible de novo). HGPRT loss → brain cannot maintain nucleotide pool → dopaminergic neuron dysfunction → movement disorder + self-mutilation behaviour.

ADA Deficiency → SCID PYQ 2011-S
  • Deficiency: Adenosine Deaminase (ADA)
  • Inheritance: Autosomal recessive
  • ~15% of all SCID cases

Biochemical Mechanism:

  • ADA absent → deoxyadenosine → phosphorylated to dATP accumulates
  • ↑↑ dATP → inhibits ribonucleotide reductase
  • Ribonucleotide reductase inhibited → all dNTPs ↓ → DNA synthesis blocked
  • Lymphocytes are most affected (highest ADA activity normally)

Clinical Features:

  • ↓↓ T cells + B cells + NK cells = "Bubble boy" disease
  • Recurrent severe infections from birth
  • Failure to thrive

Treatment:

  • PEG-ADA (enzyme replacement)
  • Bone marrow transplant
  • Gene therapy — first disease treated by gene therapy (1990, Ashanti DeSilva)

Nucleotide Analogs as Drugs

Synthetic nucleotide analogs exploit the machinery of nucleotide metabolism to kill rapidly dividing cells (anticancer) or inhibit viral replication (antiviral). Several are suicide inhibitors.
Drug Analog of Target Enzyme Mechanism Use
6-Mercaptopurine (6-MP) Anticancer Hypoxanthine HGPRT (converts it), then inhibits de novo + salvage Converted to 6-TIMP → inhibits PRPP amidotransferase → ↓ de novo purine synthesis. Also incorporated into DNA/RNA Leukaemia (ALL, AML)
6-Thioguanine Anticancer Guanine HGPRT converts it; then multiple targets Incorporated into DNA → strand breaks, mismatch repair activation → apoptosis Leukaemia
5-Fluorouracil (5-FU) Anticancer Uracil Thymidylate synthase (TS) — suicide inhibitor 5-FdUMP + methylene-THF forms covalent ternary complex with TS → ↓ dTMP → ↓ DNA synthesis → "thymineless death" Colon, breast, head-neck Ca
Methotrexate (MTX) Anticancer Folate (not nucleotide) Dihydrofolate Reductase (DHFR) Inhibits DHFR → ↓ THF → ↓ one-carbon transfer → ↓ dTMP + ↓ purine synthesis (both). "Indirect" nucleotide analog Leukaemia, lymphoma, autoimmune diseases, ectopic pregnancy
AZT (Zidovudine) Antiviral Thymidine HIV Reverse Transcriptase Lacks 3'-OH → chain terminator (no next nucleotide can attach). Must be phosphorylated by cellular kinases first HIV/AIDS
Acyclovir Antiviral Guanosine (acyclic) Viral thymidine kinase (activates it) → viral DNA polymerase Selectively phosphorylated by viral TK (1000× better than cellular TK) → acyclo-GTP → chain terminator of viral DNA pol Herpes simplex, VZV
Cytarabine (Ara-C) Anticancer Cytidine (arabinose sugar) DNA polymerase Incorporated into DNA → blocks elongation. Also inhibits DNA polymerase directly AML, CLL
Ciprofloxacin Antibiotic Not nucleotide per se Bacterial DNA Gyrase (Topoisomerase II) and Topoisomerase IV Stabilises enzyme-DNA cleavage complex → double-strand breaks → bacterial cell death. No direct structural analog of nucleotide Broad-spectrum bacteria
Mycophenolate & Ribavirin Immunosuppressant Purine precursors IMP Dehydrogenase Inhibits conversion of IMP to Xanthosine-5-P → ↓ GMP synthesis in rapidly dividing B/T cells Organ transplant, Hepatitis C
Hydroxyurea Anticancer - Ribonucleotide Reductase (RNR) Inhibits conversion of ribonucleotides to deoxyribonucleotides → ↓ DNA synthesis Melanoma, Sickle Cell (↑ HbF)
Why 5-FU is a Suicide Inhibitor

5-FdUMP (active metabolite) forms a covalent ternary complex with thymidylate synthase AND N⁵,N¹⁰-methylene-THF. The enzyme is irreversibly inactivated. Because it uses the enzyme's own mechanism to trap it, this is the definition of mechanism-based (suicide) inhibition. Folate co-factor is simultaneously depleted.

EQ Classic: "Ciprofloxacin is an antibiotic but 6-MP is an anticancer drug [2023]" — They both affect DNA/nucleotide metabolism but via completely different mechanisms and targets (CIP → bacterial gyrase vs 6-MP → de novo purine synthesis + DNA incorporation).

Viva & Exam Q&A

Purine Metabolism Vivas
What are the sources of N and C atoms in the purine ring? Draw and label.PYQ 2013, 2015-S

The purine ring has 9 atoms: 4C + 5N (actually 5C + 4N in the ring). The donors:

  • Glycine: contributes C4, C5, N7 (an entire glycine molecule is incorporated)
  • Glutamine: contributes N3, N9 (amide nitrogen); also N at C2 and C6
  • Aspartate: contributes N1 (via adenylosuccinate)
  • N¹⁰-Formyl-THF: contributes C2 and C8 (one-carbon folate units)
  • CO₂: contributes C6

Mnemonic: "GAGA + CO₂" → Glycine, Aspartate, Glutamine×2 (different positions), formyl-THF (C2,C8), CO₂ (C6)

The first committed step uses PRPP as the ribose scaffold. The ring is assembled on the sugar — 10 enzymatic steps to form IMP.

What is the salvage pathway? What is its clinical significance?PYQ 2018, 2025

Salvage pathway: Recycles pre-formed purine bases (from nucleic acid degradation or diet) back to nucleotides, using PRPP as the phosphoribosyl donor.

  • HGPRT: Hypoxanthine + PRPP → IMP; Guanine + PRPP → GMP
  • APRT: Adenine + PRPP → AMP

Clinical significance:

  • Energetically cheap (no de novo 10-step pathway needed)
  • Brain survival: brain has minimal de novo capacity → depends entirely on salvage
  • HGPRT deficiency → Lesch-Nyhan syndrome (gout + neurological + self-mutilation)
  • Drug exploitation: 6-MP and 6-TG are converted by HGPRT to active forms (anti-leukemia drugs work THROUGH salvage)
  • Provides feedback regulation: salvage products (IMP, GMP, AMP) inhibit de novo synthesis
How does allopurinol lower uric acid? Why is it called a suicide inhibitor?PYQ 2012, 2019

Allopurinol (isomer of hypoxanthine with C7-N8 transposed) is acted upon by xanthine oxidase:

  1. XO oxidises allopurinol → Oxipurinol (alloxanthine)
  2. Oxipurinol binds tightly to the reduced (Mo4+) form of XO → forms long-lived inhibitory complex
  3. The enzyme's own catalytic mechanism produces the inhibitor → mechanism-based / suicide inhibition

Results:

  • ↓ conversion of hypoxanthine and xanthine to uric acid
  • Hypoxanthine + xanthine accumulate (10× more soluble than urate → safely excreted)
  • PRPP accumulates → but overall PRPP is consumed → de novo synthesis also decreases
  • Reduces serum and urinary uric acid by ~60–70%

Why does ADA deficiency cause immunodeficiency specifically (not other systems)?PYQ 2011-S

The selective lymphocyte toxicity of ADA deficiency is due to unique characteristics of lymphocytes:

  • Lymphocytes have high levels of deoxynucleoside kinase (TdK) relative to 5'-nucleotidase → deoxyadenosine is preferentially phosphorylated to dAMP → dADP → dATP
  • Other cell types have high 5'-nucleotidase activity → dephosphorylate dAMP back to adenosine (before toxic accumulation)
  • Lymphocytes also have high mitochondrial deoxyguanosine kinase sensitivity
  • dATP specifically inhibits ribonucleotide reductase → all dNTPs ↓ → S-phase DNA synthesis block → apoptosis
  • Additionally, dATP causes direct DNA strand breaks and activates apoptotic pathways

Other tissues: High 5'-nucleotidase activity rapidly dephosphorylates dAMP → no toxic accumulation → spared.

What is orotic aciduria? How do you distinguish it from OTC deficiency?Advance Topic

Both conditions → ↑ orotic acid in urine. Key differentiating features:

  • OTC deficiency: Urea cycle block → carbamoyl-P accumulates in mitochondria → overflows into cytosol → pyrimidine synthesis goes into overdrive → orotate ↑. BUT: blood ammonia ↑↑↑ (main feature). No megaloblastic anaemia (UMP synthase intact can still process orotate).
  • Orotic Aciduria Type I: UMP synthase deficiency in cytosol → orotate cannot be converted to UMP → orotate ↑. Ammonia NORMAL (urea cycle intact). Megaloblastic anaemia present (cannot make pyrimidines → ↓ DNA synthesis in RBC precursors).

Treatment difference: Orotic aciduria → uridine supplements. OTC deficiency → protein restriction + ammonia scavengers + liver transplant.

Essay Question Templates

Allopurinol lowers the uric acid concentration of blood.PYQ 2012-S, 2019
Standard Exam Answer Draft:
  1. Target: Chronic Gout is caused by hyperuricaemia, resulting in Monosodium Urate crystal precipitation in joints.
  2. Therapeutic Goal: Lower serum uric acid below 6.0 mg/dL to dissolve existing crystals and prevent new deposits.
  3. Mechanism: Allopurinol is a structural isomer of hypoxanthine. Xanthine Oxidase (XO) converts it to Oxipurinol (Alloxanthine).
  4. Inhibition Class: Oxipurinol binds tightly and irreversibly to the Mo⁴⁺ active site of XO, acting as a suicide inhibitor.
  5. Metabolic Shift: Conversion of hypoxanthine and xanthine to uric acid is blocked. Soluble hypoxanthine and xanthine accumulate and are excreted, while serum uric acid levels drop.
  6. De Novo Downregulation: Clearance of PRPP by salvage pathways reduces substrates for de novo purine synthesis, further lowering urate levels.
Gout is precipitated by alcohol intake.PYQ 2012-S, 2016-S, 2018-S, 2021
Standard Exam Answer Draft:
  1. Metabolic Block: Oxidation of ethanol by Alcohol Dehydrogenase generates NADH. High NADH/NAD⁺ ratio favors the reduction of pyruvate to lactate.
  2. Excretion Competition: Lactate accumulates and competitively inhibits the organic anion transporter (URAT1) in renal proximal tubule cells, preventing renal uric acid secretion and elevating serum urate.
  3. Purine Load: Yeast in fermented drinks (such as beer) contains high levels of guanosine, which is catabolized directly into uric acid.
  4. ATP Turnover: High acetate production in liver cells drives ATP conversion to AMP. Accumulation of AMP feeds into the purine catabolic pathway, accelerating urate synthesis.
Adenosine deaminase deficiency leads to immunodeficiency state.PYQ 2011-S
Standard Exam Answer Draft:
  1. Metabolic Defect: Absence of Adenosine Deaminase prevents the conversion of adenosine to inosine, and deoxyadenosine to deoxyinosine.
  2. Metabolite Accumulation: Deoxyadenosine accumulates and is phosphorylated by lymphoid deoxycytidine kinase to form toxic dATP.
  3. Enzyme Inhibition: High dATP binds to the activity site of Ribonucleotide Reductase (RNR), shutting down the conversion of all ribonucleotides to deoxyribonucleotides.
  4. DNA Synthesis Arrest: DNA replication is arrested in T, B, and NK cells, inducing apoptosis. This results in the profound lymphopenia characteristic of Severe Combined Immunodeficiency (SCID).
