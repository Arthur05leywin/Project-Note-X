Regulation of Purine De Novo Synthesis
  • PRPP Synthetase: Allosterically inhibited by AMP, GMP, IMP (end-product regulation).
  • Glutamine:PRPP Amidotransferase (RLS): Synergistically inhibited by AMP and GMP together. Activated by substrate PRPP.
  • Cross-Regulation: AMP synthesis requires energy from GTP; GMP synthesis requires energy from ATP. This reciprocity ensures a balanced purine nucleotide pool for DNA/RNA synthesis.
  • Site of Synthesis: Primarily the liver. Brain, RBCs, and polymorphonuclear leukocytes have low de novo capacity and rely heavily on salvage.

Clinical Core — Orotic Aciduria
  • Type I Orotic Aciduria: Inherited deficiency of the bifunctional enzyme **UMP Synthase** (OPRT and/or ODCase). Orotic acid accumulates and crystallizes in urine.
  • Clinical Triad: Megaloblastic anaemia (refractory to B12/folate, because pyrimidines are required for DNA synthesis/erythropoiesis), urinary orotic acid crystals, and physical/developmental delay.
  • Treatment: **Uridine supplementation** (bypasses the block to provide UMP/dUTP, and its feed-forward conversion to UTP directly inhibits CPS II, halting overproduction of orotate).

Purine Salvage Pathways 🔴 TIER 1

⚡
Salvage: Recycles free purine bases (from cellular turnover or diet) back into active nucleotides. It consumes significantly less metabolic energy (ATP) than the 10-step de novo pathway.
⚠️
EXAM TRAP: In Lesch-Nyhan syndrome, the severe hyperuricaemia is not just due to a failure to recycle hypoxanthine and guanine. It is critically amplified because PRPP accumulates (since it's not used by HGPRT) and directly activates Glutamine-PRPP amidotransferase, driving de novo purine synthesis to go 10x faster!

Clinical Core — Lesch-Nyhan Syndrome (LNS)
  • Enzyme Defect: Complete deficiency of **HGPRT** (Hypoxanthine-Guanine Phosphoribosyltransferase). X-linked recessive inheritance.
  • Pathophysiology: Hypoxanthine and Guanine accumulate and are converted to Uric Acid. High levels of unused PRPP stimulate the rate-limiting enzyme of the de novo pathway, causing hyper-activation of purine synthesis and massive hyperuricaemia.
  • Clinical Triad: Hyperuricaemia (tophi, gout, renal urate stones), severe neurological dysfunction (choreoathetosis, spastic cerebral palsy, intellectual disability), and **compulsive self-mutilation** (lip and finger biting).
  • Neurological Link: The brain lacks the capacity for de novo purine synthesis and is completely dependent on HGPRT salvage. Loss of salvage causes depletion of GTP and damages dopaminergic pathways in the basal ganglia.

SCID Biochemical Mechanism: ADA deficiency leads to accumulation of Deoxyadenosine, which cellular kinases phosphorylate into **dATP**. High dATP is a potent allosteric **inhibitor of Ribonucleotide Reductase (RNR)**. RNR inhibition halts the synthesis of all other dNTPs (dCTP, dGTP, dTTP) → completely blocking S-phase DNA synthesis. Lymphocytes (T, B, NK cells) are extremely sensitive and undergo rapid apoptosis, causing **Severe Combined Immunodeficiency (SCID)**.

Evolutionary Loss of Uricase

Most mammals possess the enzyme **uricase**, which converts uric acid into highly soluble, easily excretable **allantoin**. During hominid evolution, the gene encoding uricase was silenced by nonsense mutations. As a result, humans and higher primates excrete uric acid as the final product, which is susceptible to precipitation in tissues at elevated concentrations.

Gout & Hyperuricaemia 🔴 TIER 1

⚡
Gout: Inflammatory arthritis triggered by the deposition of needle-shaped **monosodium urate (MSU) crystals** in joints and soft tissues (forming tophi) due to chronic hyperuricaemia (Serum urate > 7 mg/dL).
⚠️
EXAM TRAP: Allopurinol is a suicide inhibitor, but it is NOT the active inhibitor itself. Allopurinol is first oxidized by Xanthine Oxidase to Oxipurinol (Alloxanthine), which then binds irreversibly to the reduced Mo4+ active site. This is a classic question.
Etiology of Hyperuricaemia
Overproduction (10% of cases)
• HGPRT deficiency (LNS)
• PRPP Synthetase overactivity
• Rapid cell turnover (leukaemia, tumor lysis)
• Von Gierke's (G6P accumulation redirects to PPP)
• Diet rich in purines (red meat, shellfish)
Underexcretion (90% of cases)
• Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)
• Thiazide & Loop Diuretics
• Low-dose Aspirin (blocks secretory transporters)
• Ketoacidosis or Lactic Acidosis (competition for proximal tubule secretion)
Biochemical Link — Alcohol & Gout
  • Competiton: Ethanol metabolism elevates NADH, converting pyruvate to lactate. Lactate competitively inhibits the renal urate transporter (URAT1/OAT), reducing uric acid excretion.
  • ATP Depletion: Rapid metabolism of ethanol to acetate consumes ATP, generating ADP and AMP. AMP is catabolized to uric acid, increasing the urate pool.
  • Precursors: Yeast-rich alcoholic drinks like beer contain high amounts of highly absorbable guanosine.

Nucleotide Analogs as Drugs 🔴 TIER 1

⚡
Mechanism: Synthetic nucleotide and nucleoside analogs exploit cellular nucleotide metabolism enzymes to incorporate into DNA/RNA (causing chain termination or mutations) or directly inhibit critical DNA replication enzymes in cancer or viruses.
⚠️
EXAM TRAP: 5-Fluorouracil (5-FU) and Methotrexate (MTX) both inhibit thymidine synthesis, but 5-FU is a suicide inhibitor of Thymidylate Synthase (directly blocking dTMP synthesis), whereas Methotrexate is a competitive inhibitor of Dihydrofolate Reductase (DHFR), blocking the regeneration of THF.

Viva & Exam Q&A 🔴 TIER 1

Purine Metabolism & Salvage Vivas
PYQ 2013, 2015-S What are the sources of N and C atoms in the purine ring? Draw and label.
Nitrogen Donors:
  • N1: Aspartate (amino nitrogen).
  • N3, N9: Glutamine (amide nitrogen).
  • N7: Glycine.
Carbon Donors:
  • C4, C5: Glycine (entire molecule).
  • C2, C8: N¹⁰-Formyl-tetrahydrofolate (one-carbon units).
  • C6: CO₂ (bicarbonate).
Assembly note: The purine ring is constructed directly on a pre-formed **ribose-5-phosphate** scaffold. The first committed step is the synthesis of 5-phosphoribosylamine by **Glutamine:PRPP amidotransferase** (the rate-limiting step). It takes 10 enzymatic reactions to close the imidazole and pyrimidine rings, forming IMP.
PYQ 2018, 2025 What is the purine salvage pathway? Explain its clinical significance.
Definition: The salvage pathway is a metabolic system that recycles free purine bases (Hypoxanthine, Guanine, Adenine) released from nucleic acid degradation back into active nucleotides (IMP, GMP, AMP).

Enzymatic Reactions:
  • **HGPRT:** Hypoxanthine + PRPP → IMP + PPi | Guanine + PRPP → GMP + PPi
  • **APRT:** Adenine + PRPP → AMP + PPi
Clinical & Physiological Significance:
  1. Energy Conservation: Avoids the high ATP cost of the 10-step de novo synthesis pathway.
  2. Brain Homeostasis: The brain has exceptionally low levels of de novo synthesis enzymes and is completely dependent on salvage to maintain its GTP/ATP pools. Deficiencies present with severe central nervous system lesions.
  3. Lesch-Nyhan Syndrome: Inherited absence of HGPRT causes uric acid overproduction, self-mutilation, choreoathetosis, and gout.
  4. Active Pharmacology: Immunosuppressants and antineoplastic drugs (e.g., 6-MP) require conversion by HGPRT to their nucleotide forms to exert their therapeutic effects.
PYQ 2012, 2019 How does allopurinol lower uric acid? Why is it called a suicide inhibitor?
Mechanism of Action:
  1. Allopurinol is a structural isomer of hypoxanthine.
  2. It binds to the enzyme **Xanthine Oxidase (XO)**, which oxidizes it into **Oxipurinol (Alloxanthine)**.
  3. Oxipurinol binds tightly and non-covalently to the reduced molybdenum ion (Mo⁴⁺) in the XO active site.
  4. This traps the enzyme in an inactive state, preventing the oxidation of hypoxanthine and xanthine into uric acid.
Suicide Inhibition Definition: Suicide inhibition (mechanism-based inhibition) occurs when an enzyme converts a structural analogue substrate into a reactive, high-affinity inhibitor that permanently binds and inactivates the same enzyme's active site. Because XO's own catalytic action produces the inactive complex, allopurinol is classified as a suicide inhibitor.

Outcome: Serum urate levels decline, and highly soluble hypoxanthine and xanthine accumulate and are excreted in urine.

PYQ 2011-S Why does ADA deficiency selectively destroy T and B lymphocytes?
Selectivity Mechanism:
  1. Lymphocytes have exceptionally high activity of **Deoxynucleoside Kinase** (which phosphorylates deoxyadenosine to dAMP) and low activity of **5'-nucleotidase** (which degrades dAMP).
  2. Consequently, when Adenosine Deaminase is absent, deoxyadenosine is rapidly converted to **dATP** within lymphocytes.
  3. dATP is a potent allosteric inhibitor of **Ribonucleotide Reductase (RNR)**, blocking all DNA synthesis.
  4. Other tissue cells have high 5'-nucleotidase activity and bypass this block, clearing the deoxyadenosine before toxic dATP can accumulate. Lymphocytes, lacking this pathway, die via apoptosis.
Advance Topic Contrast UMP Synthase Deficiency with OTC Deficiency.
Both diseases present with high orotic acid in urine. They are differentiated by checking blood ammonia and blood cell indices:
  • OTC Deficiency: Mitochondrial urea cycle block. Carbamoyl phosphate overflows into the cytosol, driving pyrimidine synthesis and elevating orotate. Presents with **severe hyperammonaemia** (lethargy, coma) but **normal hematocrit**.
  • UMP Synthase Deficiency (Orotic Aciduria): Cytosolic pyrimidine block. Presents with **normal blood ammonia** but **profound Megaloblastic Anaemia** due to nucleotide starvation in erythroid progenitors. Treated with oral **Uridine**.

PYQ 2012-S, 2019 Explain why Allopurinol is used in the treatment of chronic Gout.
Standard Exam Answer Draft:
  1. Target: Chronic Gout is caused by hyperuricaemia, resulting in Monosodium Urate crystal precipitation in joints.
  2. Therapeutic Goal: Lower serum uric acid below 6.0 mg/dL to dissolve existing crystals and prevent new deposits.
  3. Mechanism: Allopurinol is a structural isomer of hypoxanthine. Xanthine Oxidase (XO) converts it to Oxipurinol (Alloxanthine).
  4. Inhibition Class: Oxipurinol binds tightly and irreversibly to the Mo⁴⁺ active site of XO, acting as a **suicide inhibitor**.
  5. Metabolic Shift: Conversion of hypoxanthine and xanthine to uric acid is blocked. Soluble hypoxanthine and xanthine accumulate and are excreted, while serum uric acid levels drop.
  6. De Novo Downregulation: Clearance of PRPP by salvage pathways reduces substrates for de novo purine synthesis, further lowering urate levels.
PYQ 2012-S, 2016-S, 2018-S, 2021 Explain the biochemical rationale for alcohol-induced gouty attacks.
Standard Exam Answer Draft:
  1. Metabolic Block: Oxidation of ethanol by Alcohol Dehydrogenase generates NADH. High NADH/NAD⁺ favors the reduction of pyruvate to lactate.
  2. Excrection Competition: Lactate accumulates and competitively inhibits the organic anion transporter (URAT1) in renal proximal tubule cells, preventing renal uric acid secretion and elevating serum urate.
  3. Purine Load: Yeast in fermented drinks (such as beer) contains high levels of guanosine, which is catabolized directly into uric acid.
  4. ATP Turnover: High acetate production in liver cells drives ATP conversion to AMP. Accumulation of AMP feeds into the purine catabolic pathway, accelerating urate synthesis.
PYQ 2011-S Trace the biochemical steps from ADA deficiency to SCID.
Standard Exam Answer Draft:
  1. Metabolic Defect: Absence of Adenosine Deaminase prevents the conversion of adenosine to inosine, and deoxyadenosine to deoxyinosine.
  2. Metabolite Accumulation: Deoxyadenosine accumulates and is phosphorylated by lymphoid deoxycytidine kinase to form toxic **dATP**.
  3. Enzyme Inhibition: High dATP binds to the activity site of **Ribonucleotide Reductase (RNR)**, shutting down the conversion of all ribonucleotides to deoxyribonucleotides.
  4. DNA Synthesis Arrest: DNA replication is arrested in T, B, and NK cells, inducing apoptosis. This results in the profound lymphopenia characteristic of Severe Combined Immunodeficiency (SCID).
🔥 Exam Day High-Yield Quickfire
Rate-limiting enzyme of Purine De Novo synthesis
Glutamine:PRPP amidotransferase. Inhibited by AMP, GMP, IMP. Activated by PRPP.
Rate-limiting enzyme of Pyrimidine De Novo synthesis
Carbamoyl Phosphate Synthetase II (CPS II) in the cytosol. Inhibited by UTP. Activated by PRPP.
Active metabolite of Allopurinol
Oxipurinol (Alloxanthine), which suicide-inhibits Xanthine Oxidase at its Mo⁴⁺ site.
Target enzyme and mechanism of 5-Fluorouracil (5-FU)
Thymidylate Synthase. Acts as a suicide inhibitor to deplete cellular dTMP and cause cell death.
Mechanism of Methotrexate (MTX)
Competitive inhibitor of Dihydrofolate Reductase (DHFR). Blocks folic acid reduction, depleting THF for purine and dTMP synthesis.
Primary diagnostic marker for OTC vs UMP synthase deficiency
Blood Ammonia: Severely elevated in OTC deficiency; normal in UMP synthase deficiency (Orotic Aciduria). Megaloblastic anemia is only present in Orotic Aciduria.
Why does the brain rely on salvage pathways?
The brain has low expression of Glutamine:PRPP amidotransferase, making de novo synthesis impossible. It relies on HGPRT salvage.
