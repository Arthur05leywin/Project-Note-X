🔥 00: Last Day Revision Mode
  • Competitive Kinetics: Km increases (apparent), Vmax remains unchanged. Overcome by increasing substrate concentration.
  • Non-Competitive Kinetics: Vmax decreases, Km remains unchanged. Cannot be overcome by adding more substrate.
  • The Km Rule: Km is the substrate concentration at exactly ½ Vmax. Low Km = High Affinity (e.g., Hexokinase). High Km = Low Affinity (e.g., Glucokinase).
  • Suicide Inhibition: Allopurinol mimics a substrate, gets activated by Xanthine Oxidase, and then permanently, covalently inactivates it.
  • Lineweaver-Burk Plot: Competitive inhibitors intersect on the Y-axis (same Vmax). Non-competitive inhibitors intersect on the X-axis (same Km).
  • The LDH "Flipped" Pattern: Normally, LDH-2 > LDH-1. During a Myocardial Infarction (MI), heart tissue damage flips the ratio so that LDH-1 > LDH-2.
  • High-Yield Drug Mechanisms: Statins competitively inhibit HMG-CoA Reductase. Methotrexate competitively inhibits DHFR. Aspirin irreversibly inhibits COX-1 & COX-2.

The Big Picture 🟢 TIER 3

🩺 Why This Matters Clinically

Enzyme inhibition is not just theory — it is the basis of almost every drug in medicine. Statins inhibit HMG-CoA reductase. Methotrexate inhibits DHFR. Aspirin irreversibly inhibits COX. Allopurinol is a suicide inhibitor of xanthine oxidase. Understanding inhibition kinetics tells you how drugs work, why they fail, and how resistance develops.

The Golden Rule: In competitive inhibition — Km increases (more substrate needed to achieve half-Vmax) but Vmax remains the same because high substrate concentration can displace the inhibitor.
📊
Lineweaver-Burk Plot: Lines intersect on the Y-axis (same Vmax). X-intercept shifts toward origin → Km increased. The two lines must cross the Y-axis at the same point — if they don't, it's NOT competitive.

Km & Vmax — The Core Concept ★ EXAMINER FAV 🔴 TIER 1

📐 Michaelis-Menten Equation

v = (Vmax [S]) / (Km + [S])

Where: v = reaction velocity, Vmax = maximum velocity, [S] = substrate concentration, Km = Michaelis constant

🎯
Km Definition: Substrate concentration at which reaction velocity = ½ Vmax. It reflects the affinity of enzyme for substrate. Low Km = High affinity (less substrate needed). High Km = Low affinity.
⚡
Hexokinase vs Glucokinase (Classic PYQ!): Hexokinase has low Km for glucose — works even at low blood glucose (brain, RBC). Glucokinase has high Km — only active after a meal when glucose is high (liver, pancreatic β cells). This is why glucokinase acts as a "glucose sensor."
⚠️
EXAM TRAP: Never forget the inverse relationship of Km; a high Km means a low affinity for the substrate (like Glucokinase in the liver), it does not mean the enzyme works better or faster at basal levels.

Clinical Correlations 🔴 TIER 1

🩺 Drug–Enzyme Inhibition Pairs (High Yield)
  • Methotrexate → Competitive inhibitor of Dihydrofolate Reductase (DHFR) → Used in cancer, rheumatoid arthritis, psoriasis
  • Statins (Atorvastatin, Rosuvastatin) → Competitive inhibitor of HMG-CoA Reductase Rate Limiting Step of cholesterol synthesis → Hypolipidemia treatment
  • Allopurinol → Suicide inhibitor of Xanthine Oxidase → Reduces uric acid → Gout treatment
  • Aspirin → Irreversible inhibitor of COX-1 & COX-2 via acetylation of a specific serine residue at the active site → Anti-platelet effect lasts life of platelet (7–10 days)
  • Sulfanilamide / Sulfonamides → Competitive analog of PABA → Inhibits bacterial dihydropteroate synthase → Antibiotic
  • Penicillin → Irreversible inhibition of Transpeptidase → Blocks bacterial cell wall synthesis
  • Neostigmine → Reversible inhibitor of Acetylcholinesterase → Used in myasthenia gravis
  • Organophosphates (insecticides) → Irreversible inhibition of AChE → Toxicity: SLUDGE syndrome

Memory Mnemonics 🟢 TIER 3

View Mnemonics
🧠 Mnemonic 1
"Competitive Kills Km, Non-competitive Nails Vmax"
Competitive → Km changes (↑), Vmax stays.
Non-competitive → Vmax changes (↓), Km stays.
🧠 Mnemonic 2 — Lineweaver-Burk
"Competitive meets at Y, Non-competitive meets at X"
Competitive inhibition → lines cross at Y-axis intercept (same 1/Vmax = same Vmax).
Non-competitive → lines cross at X-axis intercept (same -1/Km = same Km).
🧠 Mnemonic 3 — CK Isoenzymes
"Brain Beats Muscles" → BB, MB, MM
CK-BB = Brain | CK-MB = Myocardium (Heart) | CK-MM = Muscles
CK-MB is the GOLD STANDARD cardiac marker in early MI (before Troponin era in many exams).

🔥 Viva Rapid Fire — Enzyme Inhibition 🟢 TIER 3
Q1 What happens to Km and Vmax in competitive inhibition?
Km increases (apparent), Vmax remains unchanged. The inhibitor competes with substrate for the active site. High substrate concentration can displace the inhibitor.
Q2 Why is allopurinol called a suicide inhibitor?
Allopurinol is first oxidized by xanthine oxidase to alloxanthine (oxypurinol), which then permanently and covalently binds to the active site of xanthine oxidase, inactivating it irreversibly. The enzyme participates in its own inhibition — hence "suicide."
Q3 Why is Km of glucokinase higher than hexokinase?
Glucokinase (hepatic) is a low-affinity, high-Km enzyme that functions as a glucose sensor — it activates only when blood glucose is high (postprandial). Hexokinase has high affinity (low Km) and works even at basal glucose levels, ensuring glucose supply to brain and RBCs at all times.
Q4 What does the Lineweaver-Burk plot show?
It's a double reciprocal plot of 1/v vs 1/[S]. Y-intercept = 1/Vmax. X-intercept = -1/Km. Slope = Km/Vmax. Used to distinguish types of inhibition: competitive lines cross at Y-axis; non-competitive lines cross at X-axis.
Q5 Name four non-functional plasma enzymes and their significance.
1) ALT/SGPT — liver damage (hepatocellular). 2) AST/SGOT — liver, cardiac damage. 3) Alkaline Phosphatase — obstructive jaundice, bone disease. 4) Amylase — pancreatitis. 5) CK-MB — Myocardial infarction. These enzymes normally function intracellularly; their appearance in plasma indicates cell damage.
Q6 Differentiate inhibitors from uncouplers (ETC context — frequent combo Q)
Inhibitors block electron transport at specific complexes (e.g., CN⁻ at Complex IV, Rotenone at Complex I) — stop both electron flow AND ATP synthesis. Uncouplers (e.g., 2,4-DNP, thermogenin) allow electron transport to continue but dissipate the proton gradient — electrons flow but NO ATP is made. Heat is generated instead.
⚠️
EXAM TRAP: Do not confuse ETC inhibitors with uncouplers in viva questions; inhibitors (like Cyanide) stop both electron flow and ATP synthesis, while uncouplers (like 2,4-DNP) allow electron flow to continue but destroy the proton gradient, creating heat instead of ATP.
