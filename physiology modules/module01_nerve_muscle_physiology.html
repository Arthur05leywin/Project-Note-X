Resting Membrane Potential

RMP of a nerve fibre = –70 mV (skeletal muscle = –90 mV, cardiac = –90 mV). Inside is NEGATIVE relative to outside. Maintained by the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump + differential membrane permeability.
Ionic Basis of RMP ⭐ PYQ 2016-S, 2023

Why is the inside NEGATIVE?

  • At rest, membrane is 30× more permeable to K⁺ than Na⁺
  • K⁺ diffuses OUT down concentration gradient → positive charges leave
  • Large anionic proteins (A⁻) trapped inside → cannot cross membrane
  • Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pumps 3Na⁺ OUT for every 2K⁺ IN → electrogenic, net negative inside
  • Nernst equation for K⁺: E_K = –94 mV (close to RMP → K⁺ is the dominant ion)

Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase Pump — Key Facts

  • Pumps 3Na⁺ OUT + 2K⁺ IN per ATP hydrolysed
  • Uses ~25–40% of cell's total ATP at rest
  • Net electrogenic contribution: –4 mV (small but critical)
  • Inhibited by: ouabain, digitalis, cyanide, hypoxia
  • Maintains cell volume by controlling osmotic balance
  • Blocked by digitalis → ↑ intracellular Na⁺ → ↑ intracellular Ca²⁺ via NCX → ↑ cardiac contractility
EXAM TRAP: RMP is NOT due to Na⁺/K⁺ pump alone. The pump maintains the concentration gradients; the actual RMP is generated by K⁺ diffusion through leak channels. If only the pump stopped, RMP would decay slowly — not vanish instantly.
Gibbs-Donnan Equilibrium PYQ 2014, 2018

When a semipermeable membrane separates two solutions and one side contains non-diffusible charged ions (e.g., protein anions), an unequal distribution of diffusible ions results. At equilibrium: [K⁺]in × [Cl⁻]in = [K⁺]out × [Cl⁻]out. This creates a Donnan potential (inside negative). Relevant for: plasma proteins and the distribution of electrolytes between plasma and interstitial fluid.

02

Action Potential

AP = a brief, self-propagating, all-or-none reversal of membrane potential. In nerve: peak = +35 mV. Duration ~1 ms. Propagates without decrement. Triggered when membrane reaches threshold (~–55 mV).
Phases of AP in Nerve ⭐ PYQ 2016-S, 2023
Action Potential Phases — Nerve Fibre
Resting Phase Membrane at –70 mV. Voltage-gated Na⁺ channels CLOSED. K⁺ leak channels open. Na⁺/K⁺ pump active.
↓ Stimulus reaches threshold (–55 mV)
Depolarisation (Rising Phase) Voltage-gated Na⁺ channels open rapidly → Na⁺ rushes IN → inside becomes positive. Peak = +35 mV (not +60 mV = Na⁺ equilibrium, because K⁺ channels begin opening)
↓ Na⁺ channel inactivation gate closes (h-gate)
Repolarisation (Falling Phase) Voltage-gated K⁺ channels open slowly → K⁺ rushes OUT → membrane returns toward –70 mV. Na⁺ channels inactivated (cannot reopen until reset).
↓ K⁺ channels slow to close
After-Hyperpolarisation (Undershoot) K⁺ channels remain open briefly → membrane overshoots to ~–80 mV → Absolute Refractory Period ends, Relative Refractory Period
↓ Na⁺/K⁺ pump restores ion gradients
Return to RMP Na⁺ channels reset (m-gate closes, h-gate opens). Membrane returns to –70 mV. Ready for next AP.
Why AP stops at +35 mV, NOT +60 mV (Na⁺ equilibrium)? Because voltage-gated K⁺ channels begin to open near the peak, pulling K⁺ out simultaneously. The two opposing currents prevent full Na⁺ equilibrium from being reached. [PYQ 2025 Group-D: "Depolarisation stops at +35 mV"]

Why Heart Cannot Be Tetanised — Clinical Relevance

If the heart could be tetanised (sustained contraction), it could never relax to fill with blood → no cardiac output → death. The long ARP is therefore a life-saving physiological feature. Tetanus can be demonstrated easily in skeletal muscle (RRP is short) but NOT in cardiac muscle. [PYQ 2019 Group-D, 2025]

03

Neuromuscular Junction

NMJ = cholinergic synapse between motor nerve terminal and skeletal muscle motor end plate. Neurotransmitter: Acetylcholine (ACh). Receptor: Nicotinic ACh receptor (nAChR) — ligand-gated Na⁺/K⁺ channel.
Structure of NMJ ⭐ PYQ 2010 Group-A, 2013-S, 2020

Pre-synaptic (Motor Nerve Terminal):

  • Synaptic vesicles: each contains ~10,000 ACh molecules → quantal release
  • Active zones: ACh release sites aligned with junctional folds
  • Voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels (P/Q-type): AP arrives → Ca²⁺ enters → triggers vesicle fusion (SNARE proteins: synaptobrevin, syntaxin, SNAP-25)
  • Mitochondria: rich supply for ACh synthesis and ATP

Post-synaptic (Motor End Plate):

  • Junctional folds: increase surface area for nAChR
  • nAChR: pentameric ligand-gated channel (α₂βγδ or α₂βεδ) — 2 ACh molecules bind to 2 α-subunits to open
  • Acetylcholinesterase (AChE): in synaptic cleft, rapidly hydrolyses ACh → choline + acetate (terminated in <1 ms)
  • Choline re-uptake by hemicholinium-sensitive transporter
NMJ Transmission — Step by Step
Step 1 — AP arrives at motor nerve terminal Depolarisation opens voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels (P/Q-type) → Ca²⁺ flows INTO terminal
↓ Ca²⁺ triggers SNARE complex (synaptotagmin acts as Ca²⁺ sensor)
Step 2 — Quantal release of ACh Synaptic vesicles fuse with presynaptic membrane → ACh released into synaptic cleft (~50–100 quanta per impulse)
↓ ACh diffuses across 50 nm synaptic cleft
Step 3 — Receptor binding → End Plate Potential (EPP) 2 ACh molecules bind 2 α-subunits of nAChR → channel opens → Na⁺ flows IN + K⁺ flows OUT → EPP generated (depolarisation of end plate to ~–50 mV)
↓ EPP reaches threshold → triggers AP in muscle
Step 4 — Muscle AP and Termination Muscle AP generated → spreads along sarcolemma. Meanwhile, AChE hydrolyses ACh in <1 ms → channels close → EPP terminated
↓ Choline taken back up by nerve terminal → resynthesised into ACh (ChAT enzyme, needs acetyl-CoA)

04

Skeletal Muscle Contraction

Skeletal muscle contraction follows the Sliding Filament Theory (Huxley & Hanson, 1954). Thick filaments (myosin) slide over thin filaments (actin) — filament lengths do NOT change, but sarcomere shortens.
Sarcomere Structure & Banding ⭐ PYQ 2024 Group-B

Bands & Lines:

  • A band (Anisotropic, dark): Thick myosin filaments + overlapping actin. Does NOT shorten.
  • I band (Isotropic, light): Thin actin only. SHORTENS on contraction.
  • H zone: Central A band, myosin only (no actin). SHORTENS on contraction.
  • M line: Centre of H zone — connects myosin filaments.
  • Z line (Zwischenscheibe): Anchors actin, defines sarcomere boundary. MOVES CLOSER together.

Contractile Proteins:

  • Myosin (thick filament): Heavy chain (rod + S1 head with ATPase activity + actin-binding site) + Light chains (regulatory). 300 myosin molecules per thick filament.
  • Actin (thin filament): F-actin (polymer of G-actin). Contains binding sites for myosin heads.
  • Tropomyosin: Coiled-coil protein that blocks myosin-binding site on actin at rest.
  • Troponin complex: TnT (attaches to tropomyosin), TnI (inhibitory), TnC (binds Ca²⁺ → causes conformational change → removes tropomyosin block).
  • Titin: Elastic protein, anchors myosin to Z line, responsible for passive tension.
Sliding Filament Theory — Cross-Bridge Cycle
Step 1 — Energised (Cocked) Myosin Head ATP binds myosin S1 head → hydrolysed to ADP + Pi → head "cocks" into 90° position (HIGH ENERGY state)
↓ Ca²⁺ removes tropomyosin inhibition (see E-C coupling)
Step 2 — Cross-Bridge Formation Energised myosin head binds to actin (weak binding initially → strong binding after Pi release)
↓ Pi released → strong binding
Step 3 — Power Stroke (Force Generation) Myosin head pivots from 90° → 45° (power stroke) → actin pulled toward M line by ~10 nm. ADP released.
↓ ADP released; rigor state (no ATP)
Step 4 — Detachment New ATP binds myosin → detachment from actin. ATP hydrolysed → head recocks. Cycle repeats if Ca²⁺ still present.
↓ Without ATP → cross-bridges remain locked = RIGOR MORTIS
KEY EXAM POINT: ATP is needed for BOTH contraction (power stroke, repeated cycling) AND relaxation (detachment of cross-bridges + SERCA pump to resequester Ca²⁺). "Relaxation of muscle requires energy" [PYQ 2015-S, 2023 Group-D]

Excitation-Contraction Coupling

E-C Coupling = the sequence of events linking muscle AP (electrical event) to cross-bridge cycling (mechanical event). Key player: Ca²⁺. Key structures: T-tubules, Sarcoplasmic Reticulum (SR), DHPR, RyR1.
Excitation-Contraction Coupling — Skeletal Muscle
AP spreads along sarcolemma → enters T-tubules T-tubules (transverse tubules) are invaginations of sarcolemma that penetrate deeply into muscle fibre → AP reaches the triad (T-tubule + 2 SR cisternae)
↓ DHPR senses voltage change
DHPR (Dihydropyridine Receptor = L-type Ca²⁺ channel) activation In skeletal muscle, DHPR acts as a VOLTAGE SENSOR (mechanical coupling, not Ca²⁺ entry like in cardiac). Conformational change in DHPR → physically opens RyR1.
↓ RyR1 opens on SR terminal cisternae
Ca²⁺ release from SR Massive Ca²⁺ release → cytosolic [Ca²⁺] rises from 0.1 µM → 10 µM. Ca²⁺ binds TnC → conformational change → tropomyosin moves → myosin-binding sites on actin EXPOSED.
↓ Cross-bridge cycling (see S4)
Relaxation — Ca²⁺ Resequestration SERCA pump (SR Ca²⁺-ATPase) actively pumps Ca²⁺ back into SR against gradient (ATP-dependent). [Ca²⁺] falls → TnC loses Ca²⁺ → tropomyosin re-blocks actin → contraction ends.
Skeletal vs Cardiac E-C Coupling: In skeletal muscle, DHPR works by MECHANICAL coupling (no Ca²⁺ entry needed from outside). In cardiac muscle, Ca²⁺ enters via DHPR → triggers Ca²⁺-induced Ca²⁺ release (CICR) from RyR2. This is why hypocalcaemia does NOT affect skeletal muscle contraction (no extracellular Ca²⁺ needed) but DOES affect cardiac. [PYQ 2023 Group-D]

Rigor Mortis & Tetanus

Rigor Mortis ⭐ PYQ 2011, 2014-S, 2019

Definition: Stiffening of muscles after death, caused by permanent cross-bridge formation due to depletion of ATP.

Mechanism:

  • After death: ATP production ceases (no oxidative phosphorylation, glycolysis)
  • Without ATP: myosin heads cannot DETACH from actin (no Step 4 of cross-bridge cycle)
  • Ca²⁺ leaks from SR (cannot be pumped back without ATP)
  • Ca²⁺ activates troponin → cross-bridges form and LOCK → rigor
  • Muscle STIFF and INEXTENSIBLE

Timeline & Resolution:

  • Onset: 3–6 hours after death (ATP residual depleted)
  • Fully established: 12 hours
  • Resolves: 48–72 hours (proteolytic enzymes from lysosomes digest muscle proteins)
  • Affected first: small muscles (face, jaw), then large (limbs)
  • Medico-legal importance: used to estimate time of death
Rigor mortis ≠ Contracture. Rigor = permanent, no electrical activity. Contracture = sustained contraction WITH electrical activity (e.g., in myotonia). Also: Rigor mortis is worsened by warm environment (faster ATP depletion) and delayed in cold (preserves ATP).

Myasthenia Gravis & Lambert-Eaton

Myasthenia Gravis (MG) ⭐ PYQ 2010, 2011, 2014, 2023 Group-B

Pathophysiology:

  • Autoimmune: IgG antibodies against nAChR α-subunit (postsynaptic)
  • Antibodies cause: (1) receptor blockade, (2) receptor degradation (↑ endocytosis), (3) complement-mediated destruction of end plate
  • Result: ↓ functional nAChR → EPP below threshold → muscle fails to contract
  • Safety factor is reduced → first few APs generate weak contractions; muscle fatigues rapidly with repeated stimulation
  • EMG: decremental response on repetitive stimulation (amplitude decreases)

Clinical Features:

  • Fatigable weakness: worse with use, better with rest
  • Ptosis (drooping eyelid), diplopia (early)
  • Dysphagia, dysarthria, facial weakness
  • Proximal limb weakness
  • Myasthenic crisis: respiratory failure (life-threatening)
  • Associated with thymoma in 10–15%
  • Diagnosis: Edrophonium (Tensilon) test — short-acting AChE inhibitor → transient improvement

Glucose in ORS co-transports Na⁺ via SGLT1 (secondary active transport) in intestinal mucosa. This Na⁺ absorption drives osmotic water reabsorption. Viva & Exam Q&A

RMP, Action Potential & Conduction
What is RMP? Explain its ionic basis.PYQ 2016-S, 2023

RMP of nerve = –70 mV (inside negative relative to outside).

Basis:

  • Membrane at rest: 30× more permeable to K⁺ than Na⁺ (via leak channels)
  • K⁺ diffuses out → positive charges leave → inside becomes negative
  • Large intracellular anions (proteins, A⁻) cannot cross → trapped inside → contribute to negativity
  • Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase: pumps 3Na⁺ out + 2K⁺ in → electrogenic contribution (–4 mV) + maintains gradients
  • Equilibrium for K⁺ (Nernst potential) ≈ –94 mV; for Na⁺ ≈ +60 mV
  • Actual RMP = weighted average of all ion equilibrium potentials (Goldman equation)

Key exam point: K⁺ is the DOMINANT ion determining RMP. Na⁺/K⁺ pump MAINTAINS the gradients but is not the primary generator of RMP.

Why does the AP stop at +35 mV and not reach Na⁺ equilibrium of +60 mV?PYQ 2025

Because voltage-gated K⁺ channels begin to open near the peak of depolarisation. As Na⁺ rushes in and membrane reaches +35 mV, K⁺ channels are already opening and K⁺ starts flowing OUT. This opposing outward K⁺ current prevents the membrane from reaching full Na⁺ equilibrium. Additionally, Na⁺ channel inactivation (h-gate closure) also limits further Na⁺ entry.

Why is conduction faster in thicker nerve fibres?PYQ 2013-S

Two reasons:

  • Lower internal resistance: Thicker diameter → larger cross-sectional area → less resistance to longitudinal current flow → current spreads further ahead → more of the axon reaches threshold in one step
  • Saltatory conduction (in myelinated fibres): Current jumps from node of Ranvier to node, skipping internodal segments (insulated by myelin) → much faster than point-to-point conduction

Example: Aα fibre (12–20 µm, myelinated) conducts at 70–120 m/s vs C fibres (0.2–1 µm, unmyelinated) at 0.5–2 m/s.

NMJ, Muscle Contraction & NM Blockers
Describe the sequence of events at the NMJ from AP to muscle contraction.PYQ 2010, 2013-S, 2020

1. AP arrives at motor nerve terminal → VG Ca²⁺ channels (P/Q-type) open → Ca²⁺ enters

2. Ca²⁺ triggers SNARE complex → ACh vesicles fuse → ACh released into cleft (~quantal)

3. ACh binds 2 α-subunits of nAChR → channel opens → Na⁺ in + K⁺ out → EPP generated

4. EPP exceeds threshold → AP propagates along sarcolemma + into T-tubules

5. T-tubule AP → DHPR (voltage sensor) → mechanically opens RyR1 → Ca²⁺ released from SR

6. Ca²⁺ binds TnC → tropomyosin moves → actin binding sites exposed → cross-bridge cycling → contraction

7. AChE hydrolyses ACh → EPP terminates; SERCA pump resequesters Ca²⁺ → relaxation

Note the "safety factor" — EPP is normally well above threshold, so each nerve AP reliably produces a muscle AP.

Why does relaxation of muscle require ATP? Why is rigor mortis a permanent state?PYQ 2015-S, 2018, 2019, 2023

Relaxation needs ATP for TWO reasons:

  • Cross-bridge detachment: New ATP must bind the myosin head to detach it from actin (Step 4 of cross-bridge cycle). Without ATP, cross-bridges remain locked (rigor)
  • SERCA pump: Ca²⁺ must be pumped back into SR against concentration gradient → requires ATP hydrolysis

Rigor Mortis: After death, ATP production stops. Existing ATP depleted in ~3–6 hours. Without ATP, myosin heads cannot detach from actin → permanent cross-bridge lock → stiffness. Eventually resolves (48–72 hr) when lysosomal proteases autolyse the muscle proteins.

Differentiate Myasthenia Gravis from Lambert-Eaton Syndrome.PYQ 2010 Group-A, 2023

Key differences:

  • Antibody target: MG → post-synaptic nAChR; LEMS → pre-synaptic VGCC (P/Q-type)
  • EMG: MG → decremental; LEMS → incremental (facilitating) on rapid repetitive stimulation
  • Exercise effect: MG → worse with exercise; LEMS → brief improvement with exercise
  • Association: MG → thymoma; LEMS → small cell lung cancer (paraneoplastic)
  • Autonomic features: MG → absent; LEMS → dry mouth, impotence, constipation
  • Treatment response: MG → responds well to AChE inhibitors; LEMS → poor response to AChE inhibitors, uses 3,4-DAP
Differentiate depolarising from non-depolarising NM blockers. Which is reversed by neostigmine?PYQ 2012, 2016, 2017

Depolarising (Succinylcholine): Activates nAChR → sustained end plate depolarisation → fasciculations → flaccid paralysis. NOT reversed by neostigmine (more ACh worsens Phase II block). Reversed spontaneously when pseudocholinesterase metabolises it.

Non-depolarising (d-TC, vecuronium, etc.): COMPETES with ACh for nAChR but does NOT activate it → no fasciculations → flaccid paralysis. REVERSED by neostigmine (↑ ACh → outcompetes blocker).

Clinical tip: Always administer glycopyrrolate (anticholinergic) with neostigmine to prevent muscarinic side effects (bradycardia, salivation) when reversing NM block.

Ion Transport & Applied
Why does hypocalcaemia NOT affect skeletal muscle contraction but DOES affect cardiac?PYQ 2023

Skeletal muscle: E-C coupling is mechanical (DHPR → RyR1 via direct conformational coupling). Ca²⁺ comes entirely from SR. No extracellular Ca²⁺ is needed for contraction. Therefore, hypocalcaemia does NOT affect force of contraction.

However, hypocalcaemia INCREASES neuronal excitability (Ca²⁺ stabilises Na⁺ channels → less Ca²⁺ = lower threshold) → spontaneous APs → tetany (involuntary muscle spasms).

Cardiac muscle: E-C coupling depends on Ca²⁺-induced Ca²⁺ release (CICR). Extracellular Ca²⁺ enters via L-type DHPR → triggers RyR2 → SR Ca²⁺ release. Lower extracellular Ca²⁺ → less trigger Ca²⁺ → weaker CICR → reduced force of contraction.

Why does digitalis increase cardiac contractility?PYQ 2017 Group-D

Digitalis (ouabain, digoxin) inhibits Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase → Na⁺ accumulates inside cell → Na⁺ gradient decreases → Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger (NCX) has less driving force to expel Ca²⁺ → intracellular Ca²⁺ rises → more Ca²⁺ available for SR loading → stronger contraction (positive inotropy).

Clinically used in heart failure and atrial fibrillation. Toxic dose → dangerous hyperkalaemia and arrhythmias.

EQ — Describe NMJ + AP Generation in Muscle + Myasthenia Gravis [PYQ 2010 Group-A]
"Describe the NMJ with diagram. How does an AP in the motor nerve produce an AP in muscles? What is Myasthenia Gravis and Lambert-Eaton Syndrome?"
  1. Structure of NMJ: Motor nerve terminal (synaptic vesicles with ACh, active zones, VG Ca²⁺ channels) → synaptic cleft (50 nm, contains AChE) → motor end plate (junctional folds, nAChR, high mitochondrial density). Draw and label.
  2. Mechanism of transmission: AP → Ca²⁺ entry → quantal ACh release → binds nAChR → EPP → muscle AP → E-C coupling → contraction. AChE rapidly terminates signal.
  3. Myasthenia Gravis: Autoimmune (IgG anti-nAChR antibodies) → ↓ functional receptors → EPP below threshold → fatigable weakness. EMG: decremental response. Treat with AChE inhibitors, immunosuppression, thymectomy.
  4. Lambert-Eaton: Autoimmune (anti-VGCC antibodies) → ↓ Ca²⁺ entry → ↓ ACh release. EMG: incremental response. Associated with SCLC. Treat with 3,4-DAP.
  5. Key difference: MG = post-synaptic; LEMS = pre-synaptic. MG = worse with exercise; LEMS = briefly better.
EQ — Sarcomere + Sliding Filament Theory [PYQ 2024 Group-B]
"What is a sarcomere? Describe banding pattern. Briefly describe mechanism of contraction with flowchart."
  1. Sarcomere definition: Functional and structural unit of skeletal muscle, bounded by two Z lines. Contains thick (myosin) and thin (actin) filaments arranged in parallel.
  2. Banding pattern: A band (dark, myosin ± actin), I band (light, actin only, shortens), H zone (myosin only, shortens), M line (centre), Z line (anchors actin, moves closer).
  3. Proteins: Thick = myosin (S1 head has ATPase + actin-binding). Thin = actin + tropomyosin + troponin (TnT, TnI, TnC). Titin (elastic). Nebulin (template for actin).
  4. Mechanism — cross-bridge cycle: (a) ATP → ADP+Pi, head cocks; (b) Ca²⁺ removes tropomyosin block; (c) head binds actin (strong bond after Pi release); (d) Power stroke: 90°→45° rotation, ~10 nm movement; (e) New ATP binds → detachment → recocking. Cycle repeats while Ca²⁺ present.
  5. Relaxation: SERCA pump resequesters Ca²⁺ (ATP-dependent). TnC loses Ca²⁺ → tropomyosin re-blocks → cross-bridges stop cycling.
EQ — "Relaxation of Muscle Requires Energy" [PYQ 2015-S, 2023]
"Justify: Relaxation of muscle requires energy."
  1. The intuitive error: Students often think only contraction requires ATP (for cross-bridge cycling). But relaxation is equally energy-dependent.
  2. Reason 1 — Cross-bridge detachment: After the power stroke, the myosin head remains ATTACHED to actin (rigor state) until a new ATP molecule binds. ATP binding → weak binding → detachment → head can re-cock. Without ATP: permanent attachment = rigor mortis.
  3. Reason 2 — SERCA pump: To relax, [Ca²⁺] must fall below 0.1 µM. SERCA (SR Ca²⁺-ATPase) pumps Ca²⁺ back against a 10,000:1 concentration gradient into SR lumen. This requires direct ATP hydrolysis.
  4. Reason 3 — Plasma membrane NCX and PMCA: A small amount of Ca²⁺ exits via the NCX (secondary active) and PMCA (primary active, ATP-dependent) to maintain long-term ionic balance.
  5. Clinical proof: In rigor mortis, ATP depletion leads to permanent cross-bridge attachment → stiffness = failure of RELAXATION, not contraction.
